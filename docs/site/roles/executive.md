## Who this is for

Sponsors of architecture projects, members of the architecture board and steering committees, and leaders who rely on
the architecture to decide: chief officers, heads of business units, portfolio and programme leads. You rarely change a
model; you read what it says, ask questions of it, and approve what is put in front of you.

## A day in your life

Your time goes on decisions. Before a steering committee you want one page that says where the transformation stands,
which deliverables are signed off and which are stuck, and what the open risks are. A request arrives to approve a
project charter you sponsor; another asks the board to accept a dispensation from a standard. Between meetings someone
asks what retiring the old policy administration system would touch, and you want a picture, not a week of analysis.

## Where EA Explorer fits

EA Explorer turns the architecture into answers you can trust and act on. Dashboards and KPIs show the state of the
estate and its trend; each project's report shows every phase and deliverable at a glance; impact graphs and heat maps
answer "what if" in minutes. Approvals reach you in one list, with the evidence and reasoning beside them, and your
decision is recorded for the record. You need no training in modelling: you read, ask and decide. See
[[enterprise-architecture]], [[governance-and-sign-off]] and [[views-and-reports]].

## Where you sit in TOGAF

You are the sponsor and the governance. In the ADM's phase A you agree the vision and approve the statement of architecture work; through the architecture board and sign-off you approve deliverables and decide exceptions; in phases G and H you decide when change is enough to start again. See [[ea-explorer-and-togaf]].

## How it is put together

The architecture lives in **models**; change happens through **projects** that follow the TOGAF ADM
([[the-adm]], [[kinds-of-projects]]). Each project has a **charter** its sponsor approves, **deliverables** its
sign-off people approve, and a **governance log** of the decisions on the way. As sponsor or board member you are one
of those approvers: what you approve, reject or comment is kept with the work.
