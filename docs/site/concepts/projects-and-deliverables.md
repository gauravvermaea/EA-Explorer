## A project is the work around the models

The models hold what the architecture *is*. A project holds the work of *changing* it: why (the charter and goals),
for whom (stakeholders and their key questions), how (the ADM phases and steps), what comes out (deliverables and
artifacts) and who agreed (sign-off and the governance log). Keeping these together means anyone can later see not
only the target architecture but how and why it was chosen.

## The parts

- **Charter and scope**: the sponsor's agreement to what the project is for and what it covers. See [[write-the-project-charter]].
- **Models**: the baseline, the target and any transition or scenario models, each with its role. See [[add-models-to-a-project]].
- **Stakeholders and key questions**: who cares, what they need to know, and the views that answer it. See [[stakeholders-and-key-questions]].
- **Phases and steps**: the [[the-adm|ADM]] as tailored for this project.
- **Deliverables**: documents and artifacts from the template library, checked out and in as versions.
- **Principles and standards**: the rules the project follows. See [[principles-and-standards]].
- **Gaps, work packages and the roadmap**: from baseline to target. See [[make-a-gap-analysis]].
- **Governance log and sign-off**: decisions, dispensations, reviews and approvals. See [[governance-and-sign-off]].
- **Tasks, calendar and sheets**: the day-to-day running of the work.

## From start to finish

A project is started from a template or blank, run phase by phase, and **finished** when every deliverable is signed
off: then nothing more changes and it becomes part of the record. Projects can be backed up and restored, derived from
one another, and made into templates or library projects others start from.
