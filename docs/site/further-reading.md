## EA Explorer itself

- [The EA Explorer YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@ea-explorer-os): short videos by role.
- [The code on GitHub](https://github.com/gauravvermaea/EA-Explorer): the source, the releases and how to report a
  problem or suggest a change.

## The standards it follows

- [The TOGAF Standard](https://www.opengroup.org/togaf), from The Open Group: the Architecture Development Method (ADM)
  that EA Explorer's projects follow, and the content metamodel its models can use.
- [ArchiMate](https://www.opengroup.org/archimate-forum/archimate-overview), from The Open Group: the modelling
  language EA Explorer's metamodel and drawn views use.
- [The Model Context Protocol](https://modelcontextprotocol.io): how your own AI assistant talks to EA Explorer.
- [OData](https://www.odata.org): the feed Power BI and Excel read EA Explorer's models through.
- [OpenAPI](https://www.openapis.org): how EA Explorer's REST API is described (its own pages are at `/api/docs` in
  every installation).

## The open-source parts it is built on

- [PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org): the database.
- [Keycloak](https://www.keycloak.org): sign-in, single sign-on and multi-factor sign-in.
- [Apache Superset](https://superset.apache.org): the reports and dashboards.
- [Mayan EDMS](https://www.mayan-edms.com): document management in projects.
- [draw.io](https://www.drawio.com): the drawn views.
- [Docker](https://docs.docker.com): what every part runs in.
- [Jenkins](https://www.jenkins.io): the operations pipelines.

Every part and its licence is listed in EA Explorer itself, on System Admin › Settings › Licences.
