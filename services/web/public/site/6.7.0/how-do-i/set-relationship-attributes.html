Set a relationship's attributes

For: Practising architects

Values on the relationship itself, such as a protocol or a port.

Why do this

Some facts belong to a relationship, not to either end: the protocol of an interface, the share of a process an application supports. Relationship attributes hold them. See Metamodels: the shape of a model.

Content work is the everyday heart of architecture: recording what exists and what should, and how it all connects. EA Explorer checks it against the metamodel as you go and keeps it as your change until it is reviewed.

In TOGAF terms: Phases B, C and D: recording the building blocks and relationships of the baseline and target architectures. See EA Explorer and the TOGAF Standard.

The goal: A relationship carries its own details.

Before you start You need a role on the model that may change content (Architect or higher).

Steps

  1. Open the relationship (from either entity).
  2. Relationship attributes: type or pick each value as key : value; a * marks a required one.
  3. Save. Leave a value empty to clear it.
