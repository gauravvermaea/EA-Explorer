Who this is for

People with the Model Admin role on a model (given on that model's People tab), and the Model Sub Admin who helps them. A Model Creator (a global role) may make new models; a Library Admin looks after the model library.

A day in your life

You own one or more models, and a typical day is about keeping them trustworthy. You review and commit the changes architects made yesterday, answer a request to give a new colleague the Architect role, glance at the quality score and chase the owners still missing, and accept a delivery the application inventory sent overnight. Now and then a project needs a working copy of your model, or a child comes back to be merged, or the metamodel needs a new attribute and you open a draft for it.

Where EA Explorer fits

Your models are the shared memory of the organisation's architecture; you are their custodian. EA Explorer gives you control over who changes what, review before anything counts, a quality score with the rules behind it, and every version kept so nothing is ever lost. It handles the mechanics of copying, splitting and merging so you can focus on whether the content is right. See Models: what they are and how they relate, Metamodels: the shape of a model, Data quality: why it matters and how it is measured and Versions, tags and merging.

Where you sit in TOGAF

You look after the Architecture Landscape in the Architecture Repository: the models at strategic, segment, capability and transition levels, built on the content metamodel. Your review of changes is a small piece of architecture governance, done every day. See EA Explorer and the TOGAF Standard.

How it is put together

  • A model is a set of entities (a capability, an application, a node...) and the relationships between them, each of a type its metamodel allows. Every entity has an ID (for example APP-0012), its attributes, its history and its links.
  • The metamodel says what a model may hold: domains (Strategy, Business, Application, Data, Technology and so on), entity types with their attributes, relationship types and which are allowed between which types (with how many), pick lists and validation rules. Every change to it is a published version with a note; children can be offered the new version (Push down).
  • Changes are private until committed: what an Architect changes is their uncommitted change until a Committer (or the Architect, when allowed) commits it. Conflicts show at commit. Every commit can be rolled back.
  • Tags are named moments of a model, to compare with, copy from or go back to.
  • Families: a model can be a copy (on its own) or a child (it keeps its parent; its changes go back as a merge request, and the parent's changes can be pushed down to it).
  • Model roles, highest first: Model Admin, Model Sub Admin, Committer, Architect, History Viewer, Report Viewer.
