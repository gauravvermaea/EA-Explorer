Metamodels: the shape of a model

For: Model Admin, Industry model makers, Practising architects, Developers

What a metamodel is, why every model has one, the kinds EA Explorer offers, and how one changes safely.

What a metamodel is

A metamodel is the model of a model: it says what a model may contain. It lists the entity types (Capability, Application Component, Business Process...), the attributes each type has (owner, cost, lifecycle status, a pick list of values), and the relationships allowed between types (an Application Component may serve a Business Process; a Capability may be composed of other Capabilities). It also groups types into domains (business, data, application, technology, motivation) with a colour each.

Why it matters

The metamodel is what makes a repository more than a drawing tool. Because every model follows its metamodel:

  • people put the same kind of thing in the same place, so a question can be answered across the whole model;
  • only meaningful relationships can be drawn, so impact analysis follows real dependencies;
  • reports, views and data quality rules can be written once and work on every model built on the same metamodel;
  • data from other systems can be checked before it loads.

A loose metamodel lets anything in and answers little; a strict one answers a lot but asks more of the people filling it in. Choosing the balance is the Model Admin's job.

The kinds you will meet

  • ArchiMate 4: The Open Group's modelling language, a rich general-purpose metamodel.
  • The TOGAF content metamodel: the entities the TOGAF Standard's deliverables talk about.
  • Industry metamodels: banking, insurance, government and others, from the Industry models and the library in the library.
  • Your own: built in Excel (Build a metamodel in Excel) or by extending another (Extend a model from another).

Models made from the same root share its metamodel; a child model inherits its parent's and can take its later changes (Take changes from the parent).

Changing a metamodel safely

A change to a metamodel changes what everyone may record, so it is reviewed: it starts as a draft (Start a metamodel draft), is reviewed and accepted (Review a metamodel draft), and becomes a new version of the metamodel with its note. Any two versions can be compared. See Change a model's metamodel.
