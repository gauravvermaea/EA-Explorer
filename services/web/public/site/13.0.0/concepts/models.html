Models: what they are and how they relate

A model is a body of knowledge

A model in EA Explorer is not a diagram; it is everything known about one scope of the organisation, held as entities and relationships on a metamodel. Any number of diagrams, catalogues and reports can be drawn from the same model, and they never disagree, because they read the same content.

The kinds of model

  • Root metamodels: a metamodel with no content, the start of a family (ArchiMate 4, TOGAF, an industry's own).
  • Library models: reference or template models offered to everyone: the shape of a bank, a standard set of capabilities. Anyone may copy one or make a child of it. See Industry models and the library.
  • Project models: the working models people fill in, usually created for or used by an architecture project.

Families: parents and children

A model can be a child of another. The child is a working copy: it can be changed freely, then merged back into the parent when the work is agreed (Merge a child model back), and it can take the parent's later changes. A separate copy has no such link. Families let several teams work at once without stepping on each other, and let a project build its target state beside the live baseline.

Architecture levels

Each model sits at a level of the Architecture Landscape: strategic (the long view of the whole enterprise), segment (one area, such as claims), capability (one capability in detail) or transition (a state between today and the target). The level decides where the model appears in the The Architecture Repository.

Who looks after a model

Every model has at least one Model Admin, who decides who may do what on it, keeps its metamodel and quality, and runs its wizards. See Roles and access.
