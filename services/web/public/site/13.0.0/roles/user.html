Who this is for

Everyone who works with the architecture day to day: Architects (change content), Committers (review and commit changes), History Viewers and Report Viewers (read and report), and the people on projects (Architect, Stakeholder). What you may do depends on your roles on each model and project: what you may not use is not shown.

A day in your life

You are the architect doing the work. In a day you might update the applications that changed after last week's release, map how a new process uses three systems, draw a view for a design review, answer a stakeholder's question about what retiring a platform would affect, and draft the target architecture for your project. You commit your changes with a message saying why, and your Committer reviews them.

Where EA Explorer fits

EA Explorer is your workbench and your evidence. It holds the entities and relationships you know, checks them against the metamodel as you save, and turns them into the views, matrices, heat maps and impact graphs your audience needs, always consistent with each other. When someone asks "what happens if...", the answer is a few clicks away, and your own AI assistant can explore and draft with you. See What enterprise architecture is for, Views, reports and KPIs and AI in EA Explorer.

Where you sit in TOGAF

You do the content work of the ADM: describing the baseline and target in phases B (business), C (data and applications) and D (technology), analysing the gaps in phase E, and producing the views that address stakeholders' concerns. See EA Explorer and the TOGAF Standard.

How it is put together

  • Entities and relationships: every element of the architecture (a capability, a process, an application, a server...) is an entity of a type; relationships join them, only in the ways the metamodel allows. Each entity has an ID, attributes, links to documents, a history and the views it appears in.
  • Your changes are private until committed: what you add or change is your uncommitted change, seen by you and the model's Committers, until it is committed. A Committer reviews and commits it, or sends it back.
  • Views are drawn from the repository when you open them, so they always show the data as it is now. A drawn view you make in draw.io stays in step with the model: a relationship you draw goes into the model as your change.
  • History: every change is kept, so you can see any entity or model as it was at any moment or tag.
