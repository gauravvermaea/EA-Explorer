The ADM: how architecture work is done

For: Practising architects, Executives, Project Admin, Model Admin

The TOGAF Architecture Development Method in plain words: its phases, why it is a cycle, and how a project tailors it.

The idea behind it

The Architecture Development Method (ADM) is the core of the TOGAF Standard: a repeatable way of getting from "this is how we work today" to "this is how we should work", with the reasons written down and the right people agreeing at each step. Its central idea is that change is easier to get right when you first agree why you are changing and what is in scope, then describe the business, the information and applications, and the technology, then plan the route, and only then build, while watching that what is built still matches what was agreed.

It is a cycle, not a line. Each pass teaches something, the world moves on, and the next pass starts from a better picture. Requirements sit in the middle of the cycle: every phase can raise them and every phase must respect them.

The phases

PhaseWhat it is for
PreliminarySet up the architecture practice: who does it, the principles, the framework as tailored for you
A. Architecture VisionAgree why and what: scope, stakeholders, goals, the sponsor's backing
B. Business ArchitectureHow the business works today and should work: capabilities, value streams, processes, organisation
C. Information SystemsThe data and the applications, today and in the target
D. Technology ArchitectureThe platforms, infrastructure and networks underneath
E. Opportunities and SolutionsThe gaps between today and the target, grouped into work packages
F. Migration PlanningThe order of change: transition states and a roadmap
G. Implementation GovernanceWatching delivery so it stays true to the architecture
H. Change ManagementDeciding when a change is big enough to start a new cycle
Requirements ManagementHolding every requirement through every phase

Iterations: not every project runs every phase

A project rarely needs every phase at full depth. The ADM groups the phases into iteration cycles: Architecture Capability (Preliminary and A, setting up the practice), Architecture Development (B to F), Transition Planning (E and F) and Architecture Governance (G and H). In EA Explorer you choose the cycle when you start a project (Choose the project's ADM cycle), then remove the steps that do not apply, with a reason, and add your own (Tailor a project's ADM steps). Tailoring is expected; it is how the method fits your organisation.

How EA Explorer carries it

Every project in EA Explorer follows the ADM: its phases, their steps, the deliverables each produces and who signs them off. The models hold the content (baseline and target), the project holds the work around it (charter, stakeholders, key questions, decisions), and sign-off records who agreed what and when.
