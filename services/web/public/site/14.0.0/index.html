EA Explorer

A free, open-source enterprise architecture repository you run yourself: models, views, reports, projects and AI, in one place.

EA Explorer keeps your organisation's architecture in one repository: the capabilities, value streams, processes, applications, data and technology, how they relate, and how they change over time. People draw views and run reports from it, architecture projects follow the TOGAF ADM with it, and your own AI assistant can read, analyse and change it with your permissions.

It is free and open source (AGPL-3.0), with no charge per user, per object or per module, and it runs where you choose: a laptop, one server, or a machine in your cloud.

Understand the ideas

Concepts explain the thinking behind the work: what enterprise architecture is for, the TOGAF ADM, metamodels and models, the kinds of project and their goals, governance, data quality, and how each part of EA Explorer maps to the TOGAF Standard (EA Explorer and the TOGAF Standard). Every page links to the others, as in a wiki.

Find a task

How do I lists every task, one page each, step by step. Each page prints cleanly or saves as a PDF: press Print or save as PDF at the top of the page.

Guides take a topic from start to end (making a model, restoring, merging a child model, a DR site): how its tasks fit together, each task linked to its own How do I page.

Printed guides

Printed guides has the same content as 25 guides to print and keep, each a PDF with a cover, what it is for, who it is for, a table of contents and a picture of every screen: an overview of EA Explorer and its roles; a guide for each role (System Admins, Model Admins, Project Admins, architects, industry model makers, developers and platform developers); installing, day-to-day operations, tuning, upgrades, troubleshooting and security; backups, disaster recovery, site pairing and emergencies; the eax command, Jenkins, AI and MCP, and Keycloak with your own directory; an example project from start to finish; and every error code with what to do. They are also in the repository, in the documents folder, and in EA Explorer under Documentation › Printed guides.

For developers

Integrate is for developers connecting other systems: connectors, formats, deliveries, push sources, OData in and out, reports, webhooks, the API and MCP. Platform is for developers working on EA Explorer itself: running it locally, debugging, testing and releasing.

Install it

Install and run has a guide for each set-up: a laptop, a server, any server over SSH, backups, restores, a DR site, moving machines, certificates and more. In EA Explorer the same guides are on System Admin › Tools › Setup, where you give your own values (the server's name, where the files are) and every command is filled in for you.

What is in the box

  • The metamodel: ArchiMate 4 and the TOGAF 10 content metamodel built in, or your own; edited, versioned, reviewed and pushed down to the models that use it.
  • Models: entities and relationships entered in forms and grids, loaded from Excel or CSV, or delivered by other systems; private until committed; tagged, compared, copied, split and merged back.
  • Views and diagrams: networks, impact, capability maps and heat maps, matrices, value stream maps, roadmaps and 49 standard views, all drawn from the repository when you open them; your own drawn views in the built-in draw.io.
  • Reports: 30 standard reports and dashboards, your own in the report designer, KPI trends, data quality, and an OData feed for Power BI and Excel.
  • Projects: architecture projects from 14 templates on the TOGAF 10 library (13 phases, 116 steps, 171 deliverables and artifacts), with stakeholders, key questions, principles, sign-off and documents.
  • AI: connect your own AI assistant through MCP; it works as you, with your permissions, and every change it proposes waits for your yes.
  • Running it: one command (eax) for everything, backups and restores, updates, health and alerts, a DR site kept in step, and Jenkins pipelines for the operations.
